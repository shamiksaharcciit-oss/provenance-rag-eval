# v1.8 G14 — ruling: the identity gains a second coordinate, because the domain has two

**Responds to:** STOP before Batch J. The frozen `custom_id` grammar cannot express the
call plan — `cp` makes five calls per (query, arm), `ar` nine on the targeted pair, `fa`
two across two batches — and `{metric} × {rep}` offers at most three slots. Provable, not
a judgement; three non-equivalent repairs; correctly stopped.
**Date:** 1 August 2026
**Status:** ruled as **PF-15**. Batch J may be built and submitted under it, split J1/J2.

---

## 1. The ruling: an eighth field, and a stage set of three

> `custom_id = v18-{stage}-{track}-{arm}-q{index}-{metric}-a{answer}-s{sub}`
> `{stage} ∈ {g, j1, j2}` · `{answer}` = generation rep this judgement concerns
> (0–2 on the targeted pair, 0 elsewhere) · `{sub}` = position in the metric's call plan
> (0–4 for `cp`'s per-context calls, 0–2 for `ar`'s, 0 where single).
> Worst case `v18-j1-B-u768-q149-cp-a0-s4` = 26 chars.

The two collapsed coordinates were never one thing: *which generated answer* a judgement
concerns and *which sub-call* within a metric's plan are orthogonal — `cp`'s five calls
attach to contexts and no answer; `ar`'s three attach to each answer. An identity over a
two-coordinate domain gets two fields. Every field remains a closed, hyphen-free
vocabulary, so the uniform split stays unambiguous by construction; the metric code table
remains a bijection onto the five metric names, untouched, and the bijection test stands
as written.

**Validity is derived, not enumerated in parallel:** the set of legal
(stage, metric, answer, sub) tuples is generated *from* `CALLS_PER_QUERY_ARM` and the
targeted-pair spec — the same objects that construct the requests — and the acceptance
census (pattern + length, every id, real acceptor) re-runs over that derived
cross-product. The census and the call plan therefore cannot disagree, because one is a
function of the other. Identity over assertion, applied to the census itself.

## 2. Why not the other two

Extending the metric codes (`cp0…cp4`, `ar0…ar2`) smuggles two coordinates into one field
with per-metric semantics — `cp3` means "context 3" while `ar2` means "sample 2" — and
breaks the code table's bijection for the privilege. Packing `answer × K + sub` into a
widened `rep` by divmod is arithmetic smuggling: recoverable, illegible, and a silent
contradiction of a declared closed set — the reader of a row should not need a modulus to
know what was judged. Both encode the same two coordinates this ruling makes explicit;
they just hide one.

## 3. The J1/J2 split — endorsed as forced, with one conduct clause

Faithfulness is extract-then-verify and stage 2's prompt depends on stage 1's reply, so
the split follows from PF-12 §1's own "stage order is forced by data dependency only."
Not treating it as part of the STOP was correct. `fa` extraction runs in `j1`, its
verdicts in `j2`; the (stage, metric) pairing is part of the derived validity set.

One clause so the no-peeking rule survives contact with J2: **constructing J2's prompts
requires reading J1's extraction outputs, and that reading is construction, not
peeking** — it is confined to the statement lists a verdict prompt needs, performed by
the builder, with no F1 computation, no judge-score aggregation, and no per-arm signal
assembled before the results stage. The boundary is what is *derived* from the reads:
prompts yes, numbers no.

## 4. The class — fifth instance, and this one is mine with the census in plain sight

G12's format was specified against "one call per metric." The real call plan —
`cp=5, cr=1, fa=2, ar=3, ac=1` — has sat frozen in `CALLS_PER_QUERY_ARM` since Gate 0, in
the repository, readable. I specified an identity over a domain whose definition I had
available and did not open. That is the fifth instance of the family (E1's provenance,
v1.9's budget, the id alphabet, the API pattern, now the call plan), and it sharpens the
candidate rule to its final form:

> **Every coordinate a specification quantifies over is enumerated from the artifact that
> defines it — corpus, acceptor, or call plan — never from the author's summary of it.**

Two instances of this family are now mine, two the agents', one shared. The rule goes to
the next pre-registration with five instances attached and no known counterexample.
