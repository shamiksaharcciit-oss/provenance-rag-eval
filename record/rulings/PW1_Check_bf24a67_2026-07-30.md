## 4. Two cheap machine-state checks

**Is the fault transient or persistent?** Does bge still crash under the unmonolithic path right
now? If a reboot clears it, it was transient state — which matters because a transient fault can
return mid-B5, and you would want to know that the sharded path is protecting you rather than
that the problem went away.

**Is anything else on this machine implicated?** Check Event Viewer for other `0xC0000005` faults
or WHEA entries between 07-23 and today. If there is a hardware signature, the blast radius is
wider than bge — it covers every number computed on this box, including the ones that didn't
crash. Low probability, but it is the one hypothesis whose consequences you would want to know
about before B5 rather than after, and it costs a few minutes to rule out.

Recording that you looked and found nothing is worth as much as finding something; a
`BLOCKED / ENVIRONMENT` entry with no hardware check behind it invites the question later.

## 5. A question about C-1's hoist

I had the halt gap listed as open — that was stale on my side, and you closed it at `8e3d17e`.

But the hoist raises a specific case worth checking. Before C-1, the NOT APPLICABLE path returned
`r = None` when `delta_full == 0.0`; `retention_ratio` raises `ZeroDivisionError` on the same
input. If `retention_ratio` now runs on every path, then a cell with `delta_full == 0.0` and
`full_significant` False will **halt** — and that state is entirely benign: no effect, not
significant, nothing to divide. Halting on it would be a false halt of exactly the kind the
`r > 1.0` guard exists to avoid producing.

Two possibilities, and they have opposite dispositions: either the hoist is total and you have
introduced a false halt on a benign state, or `delta_full == 0.0` still short-circuits to `None`
and the hoist is partial. Neither bites PW-1 — none of the eight frozen cells has
`delta_full == 0.0` — but the branch sweep in the test suite may reach it, and a latent false halt
in the classifier is worth ten minutes now rather than during B5.

## 6. The commit message

Falsifying your own hypothesis and saying so is the right instinct, and the specific correction —
"(c) is moot rather than unavailable" — is exactly the distinction that matters. My general lesson
about pinning survives; your inference about a version regression does not, and neither does my

One practical point: the wrong claim is in a commit message, which is durable and effectively
unamendable once pushed. A later commit saying otherwise does not travel with it — anyone reading

that travels with the record: the errata block, or the `BLOCKED / ENVIRONMENT` entry itself, which
is where a reader chasing this would actually look.

labelled hypothesis or verified.** "The published numbers came from a materially different
