## 3. The sentences that do the work

**Lead with the binary.** The ladder has two distinct levels, the primary scoring coincides with the
hostile floor, and every other choice turned out inert — so the whole design reduces to *does
inherited provenance count as a hit?* Say it in the first paragraph, because it is the shortest true
description of what PW-1 measured and it makes the rest of the document readable.

**Then give the practitioner's reading, because it is what makes the correction obviously right
rather than merely harsh.** Since S2 ≡ S3 on all eight cells, the primary number is the
strictly-contains number: the rate at which the retrieved unit *itself* holds the gold span. That is
the quantity someone building a RAG pipeline actually cares about — they are going to put the
retrieved chunk in a prompt, and a chunk credited with source text it does not contain will not help
the model answer. Framed that way, the correction is not a hostile stress test that the method
narrowly failed; it is the measurement that matches the use case, and the original figure was the
generous one.

**Give the magnitudes, they are stark and clean.** Inherited provenance accounted for 12/27 (44%) of
the measured advantage on MiniLM/A, 10/26 (38%) on bge/A, 6/10 (60%) on family 2's MiniLM/A, and
12/10 — **120%** — on bge/B, where removing it does not merely erase the advantage but reverses it.
That last one is the single most informative number in the study and it should not be buried in a
cell table.

**Name the mechanism precisely.** `formatted.py:86-94`, chunk-to-segment inheritance, 97.35% and
99.83% of excess width. The claim is not "the effect shrank under a correction" but "the measured
benefit on the size-matched control was mediated by an indexing behaviour the paper does not
document." Those are different sentences and only the second is true.

## 4. Four phrasing rules, worth stating as rules in the document itself

**The aggregate governs; the cell detail is descriptive.** Two of three applicable cells are
PARTIALLY SEPARATED with corrected deltas above a third of the original. Report that, and never with
a connective — "although", "however", "that said" — that positions it as qualifying the headline.
The least-favourable rule was frozen before these numbers existed and this is the moment it binds.

**The families do not merge.** Family 1 is a statement about the size-matched control at 256 and
says nothing about C4. Family 2 says the C4 > C0 claim is **not established and not refuted** — the
arm could not discriminate. Put one explicit sentence in the document forbidding the combination,
because a reader will make it otherwise and the table alone will not stop them.

**The two UNDERPOWERED-adjacent states stay distinct.** NO APPLICABLE CELLS means the arm was never
exercised; UNDERPOWERED means it ran and could not discriminate. You have fixed the rendering — keep
the distinction in the prose too, since that is where it will collapse first.

**Family 2's qualifier goes in the position §7 requires**, attached to the result rather than
footnoted: this is not a SEPARATED result being read as vindication, it is an arm unable to
discriminate.

## 6. Limitations — including the one that must not become a defence

PW-1 asks whether the effect survives removing inherited provenance. It does not ask whether
inherited provenance is a *valid* attribution, and it cannot: deciding that needs a different gold
span protocol, one that scores at segment granularity by design rather than by inheritance. Say so
plainly, in the limitations section, and say in the same breath that it does not modify the
headline. The burden runs the other way — a benefit visible only when a unit is credited with text
it does not contain is not a retrieval benefit in the sense the paper's readers will assume.

Two more worth stating: family 2's power at n=176 was insufficient to discriminate under correction,
which is a fact about the study's size rather than about C4; and the Track A corpus is synthetic,
so family 1's strongest cells sit on generated prose.
