# v1.8 G15 — ruling: recover the trailing JSON, uniformly, before any score exists

**Responds to:** STOP in scoring. 101 of 15,960 judge replies (0.63%) are prose followed by
valid JSON; the frozen parser refuses them; 82 cells unscoreable, unequally by arm; three
non-equivalent repairs; correctly stopped, with one conduct disclosure.
**Date:** 1 August 2026
**Status:** ruled as **PF-16**. Scoring may resume under it.

---

## 1. The ruling

`parse_json_object` is amended by ruling to a **total, uniform, content-blind syntactic
rule**: from any reply, accept exactly one trailing, complete, well-formed JSON object
whose keys match the expected schema for that call; everything before it is discarded as
reasoning. All existing strictness survives beneath that rule — no key coercion, no regex
scavenging inside objects, no repair of malformed JSON. A reply with no trailing valid
object, more than one candidate parse, or a wrong key shape still fails hard.

Conditions, all mandatory:

1. **Uniformity:** the amended parser applies to all 15,960 replies identically. On a
   conforming reply the trailing object *is* the reply, so the rule is the identity
   there — pinned by a test asserting old and new parsers agree on every conforming row.
   No already-parseable cell can change value.
2. **Outcome-blindness, preserved and stated:** the amendment is specified now, before
   `scored.json` exists — no metric value has been computed from any reply. What has been
   seen is failure *locations*, not verdict values, and the rule conditions on neither:
   it is one syntactic transformation for every reply in the run. The results document
   discloses in limitations that the parser was amended post-freeze, after failure counts
   were known and before any score existed, citing this document.
3. **The 101 stay in the sample.** This is the substantive reason recovery beats
   re-issue: the prose-prefixed replies are plausibly the judge hedging on harder cases.
   Their verdicts — the JSON the judge itself concluded with — are the draws the
   experiment paid for. Recovery keeps them; re-rolling would replace precisely the
   hardest tail with second draws, selected by the first draw's character.
4. **The hedging is data and is reported:** the 101, by metric and by arm, as a
   descriptive judge-behaviour observation — no test, no mechanism (the A-heavy split,
   80-odd against 2, is reported and not explained; A1g). A judge that disobeys "one line
   of JSON and nothing else" 0.63% of the time, concentrated in `context_recall` and
   `context_precision`, is itself a finding about judge-based evaluation, and it goes in
   the document as one.

## 2. Why not the other two

Exclusion breaks the paired family and shrinks a declared n by arm-unequal amounts —
rejected as the agent rejected it. Re-issue is outcome-dependent selection: it re-samples
only replies chosen by a property of their content, gives the hard tail a second draw, and
may simply hedge again; it also repairs nothing, so the next run inherits the defect. It
remains the fallback **only** for any reply the amended parser still refuses — of which
there are currently none (101/101 parse, schemas conform).

## 3. What the frozen strictness was for, and why this honours it

"No repair, no regex scavenging" was written to keep the scorer from *constructing* a
verdict out of ambiguous material. The trailing-object rule constructs nothing: in every
one of the 101 cases there is exactly one complete JSON object, schema-conformant, at the
position where the prompt demanded it — preceded by reasoning the prompt forbade. Taking
the judge's final object as the judge's answer is faithful to what the metric means;
refusing it because the judge also thought out loud would convert an instruction violation
by the judge into data loss for the experiment. The amendment narrows nothing the
strictness protected; ambiguity still refuses.

## 4. The bill for the abandoned probe, acknowledged

A dev-set probe with the real prompts would likely have surfaced the hedging shape before
17,642 calls were spent, and PF-16 would have been a pre-freeze amendment. The probe was
abandoned by rule at Gate 0(b) for reasons that were sound on the information available;
this is the cost side of that trade arriving, and it is recorded as such rather than
re-litigated. The general lesson joins the census file's margin note: the "real acceptor"
of a prompt specification is the model's behaviour, and it can only be censused by
spending — which is what dev probes are for.

## 5. The incidental exposure — accepted, recorded, and closed by this ruling

Reading ~10 replies' reasoning during STOP triage was reported against the no-peeking
boundary rather than absorbed, which is the conduct the rule exists to produce. Ruling:
the exposure is recorded in the results document's limitations with the affected cell ids
(the log identifies them); nothing is re-run for it. The reasoning: the boundary's purpose
is to keep human discretion from being shaped by early signal, and after this ruling **no
discretionary step remains** — the parser rule is fixed here, scoring is mechanical, and
the document assembles from frozen checklists. Scoring runs as a fresh process without
manual log reading, which is ordinary operation rather than mitigation. Ten cells of
judge prose, aggregated into nothing, with no downstream choice left to influence, is a
disclosure — not a contamination.
