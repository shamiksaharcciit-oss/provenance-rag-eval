## 1. The criterion: discretion, not subject matter

Amendment 5 says a closed document may be corrected only in the direction of claiming less. The
question is what it governs.

The line first proposed was **evidentiary claims versus a document's description of its own
contents**, on the ground that a self-description is verifiable by inspection and has no
favourable direction. That protects the right cases for a reason that does not hold: a
self-description *can* run in a favourable direction. A reproduction count is a description of
the record's contents and more of them is more apparatus validation, which a reader weighs.

The joint is not subject matter but **discretion**:

> The direction test binds where the author has discretion over the value. Where the value is
> forced, direction is irrelevant, because there is nothing for a motive to steer.

This needs no exception for self-descriptions. Their protection falls out of the fact that most
of them are forced, and the ones that are not stay governed — which is the correct result.

Worked cases. "Four" against five template amendments: forced. Count the list; there is no
judgement to make, so correcting it upward claims more and is nonetheless permitted, and a rule
that forbade it would make an undercount permanent and thereby reward undercounting. §4.1's
choice of which reproductions to cite and how to frame them: discretionary, therefore governed,
therefore declined.

---

## 2. What "forced" has to mean

A value is not forced merely because it follows from a procedure. Eleven reproductions follows
from a match criterion and a tolerance — and those were chosen. Counting a list has nothing
comparable, because there the record and the procedure are the same object.

So discretion migrates out of the value and into the criterion that produces it, and the test
must reach the criterion:

> **Forced** means forced by a procedure fixed *before the value was known.*

That is A5b's shape — one quantity, one procedure — applied to corrections rather than to tests.
The test is temporal, not epistemic: the question is whether the procedure predates the value,
not whether the author now finds the value obvious. An author who settles the procedure and the
value in the same act has exercised discretion whether or not it felt like a choice, which is
precisely why the reproduction count needed a visible correction rather than a quiet edit.

---

## 3. Correction 02 does not test this, and that is what keeps the rule clean

Correction 02 — the reproduction count, 8 to 11 — was used above as a motivating case, and its
standing was initially got wrong here. It was made in a revision that did not declare itself
final; the document closed only with the last one. **At the moment Correction 02 was made the
document was still open,** the same standing that made "Four"→"Five" an in-revision fix rather
than a reopening.

That matters in the rule's favour. Correction 02 is discretionary *and* claims more. Had it been
governed, it would be a case the direction test forbids and which was nevertheless right — a
genuine tension, and tensions get resolved by carve-outs, and carve-outs are where rules go to
die. It was not governed. So no carve-out is needed and the rule can stand at full strength:

> After closing, a discretionary correction that claims more is forbidden **even when it is
> true** — including a later-found twelfth reproduction.

Correction 02 keeps its role as the illustration of where discretion lives. It loses its role as
evidence that the direction test has an exception, because it never was.

## 4. The release valve, which the rule needs in order to survive

A rule that permanently forbids stating a true thing invites its own violation. Someone will
eventually decide the twelfth reproduction is important enough to be worth an addendum, and the
document will be reopened for a good reason, which is how every closed record gets reopened.

The valve is that **amendment 5 governs a document, not the record.** It forbids strengthening a
closed document. It does not forbid a new, dated document that states the new fact and cites the
closed one — because that leaves the closed document's persuasiveness exactly where it was at
closing, which is the whole point of the rule, while the repository as a whole still gains the
truth.

### The valve's bound

As stated so far the valve has no limit, and the addendum precedent shows what an unlimited one
becomes: *we can always add an addendum* is how a pre-registration stays negotiable after the
fact. The addendum bound was explicit — permitted only before the first arm value exists, only to
add prior knowledge, never to remove or weaken a disclosure, and anything touching a prediction
or a decision rule is a new experiment rather than an addendum. The valve needs its analogue, or
a decision simply relocates to an unfrozen artifact and the freeze buys nothing.

> **The valve carries facts, not verdicts.** A post-closing document may state a fact the closed
> document cannot, and must cite the closed document. It may **not** restate a branch, re-score a
> prediction, or alter the scope a decision was taken under. Those are a new pre-registration
> under the one-shot rule, exactly as with an addendum.

This is why a companion note stating the full 768 row is permitted although the §4.1 edit was
not. §4.1 was declined because the closed document's own claims are fixed at closing, not because
the fact may never be stated anywhere. The fact moves; the closed document does not.

**One further bound, because selection is where motive re-enters once direction is closed off.**
A note that contains only true facts can still function as a verdict revision through what it
chooses to include: the twelfth reproduction stated alone, dated after closing, reads as a
strengthening even though every word is a fact. The remedy must not be another characterisation
test — that hands the ruling back to the party with the motive. The observable form:

> A post-closing fact document must be **complete over the class it reports**, declared in the
> document. State the reproduction record as it then stands, including anything found since that
> runs the other way — never the favourable increment alone.

Completeness over a declared class is checkable against the record by inspection, which keeps
this in the same family as the direction test rather than requiring anyone to assess intent.

## 5. Item 7 — a self-description is a produced value, not a remembered one

Three failures in this programme's closing documents share one form:

| where | claim | record |
|---|---|---|
| close-out, Defect 1 | §4.3: "no values are printed" | P6 prints `D_reseam` |
| close-out, carries-forward | "Four template amendments" | five were listed |
| this note, §4 | "states no fact about v1.6" | three are stated (§1, §2, §3) |

Each is a **self-description written last, about material already present, and none survived
inspection.** Each is a universal or a count — the two forms a single counterexample falsifies
and that memory reconstructs badly. And each was written by an author who believed it accurate,
which is why "verify before commit" is the right instinct but the wrong remedy: it is an
exhortation, and all three passed the exhortation.

The mechanical form, which is the same move that fixed amendment 5 — replace a judgement with an
observable property:

> A claim a document makes about **its own contents**, where that claim is a count or a
> universal, must name the procedure that produced it — the list counted, the search run — and
> that procedure must be executed against the **final** text rather than the remembered one.

This is §2's criterion turned on the document itself. §2 says discretion migrates out of a value
and into the criterion that produces it; all three failures above are values produced by no
stated criterion at all. Naming the procedure also makes the claim checkable by a reader, who can
re-run it, which a bare absolute never is.

A weaker companion observation, offered as style rather than rule: in two of the three cases the
absolute was doing no work, and the narrower true claim served the sentence just as well. A
universal that is not load-bearing is a liability with no compensating benefit.
