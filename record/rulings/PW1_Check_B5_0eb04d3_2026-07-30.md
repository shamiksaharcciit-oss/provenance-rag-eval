## 1. The reported `r` values look like they were computed from rounded inputs

This is the fourth A1f instance, and unlike the other three it is in the *published output*. I may
be wrong about the underlying counts — check this first, it takes two minutes — but the arithmetic
is quite specific.

Every delta is a difference of recall@5 over a fixed query set, so `delta × n` is an integer. Working
back from your figures:

| cell | `delta_full` | `delta_corrected` | as counts | exact `r` | you reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MiniLM/A | +0.1534 = 27/176 | +0.0852 = 15/176 | 15/27 | **0.5556** | 0.555 |
| bge/A | +0.1477 = 26/176 | +0.0909 = 16/176 | 16/26 | **0.6154** | 0.615 |
| bge/B | +0.0667 = 10/150 | −0.0133 = −2/150 | −2/10 | **−0.2000** | −0.199 |

bge/B is the tell. `−2/10` is exactly `−0.2000`; no full-precision computation produces `−0.199`.
`0.0852/0.1534 = 0.5554` and `−0.0133/0.0667 = −0.1994` reproduce your numbers exactly, which means
the **numerator** was taken at 4 dp as well as the denominator.

You fixed the tolerance for the denominator's rounding. The same defect is sitting in the numerator,
and the fix belongs there too: compute `r` from the full-precision `delta_corrected` you already
have in memory, round once at the end. Nothing reclassifies — 0.5556, 0.6154 and −0.2000 are all far
from 0.25 and 0.75 — so this is a correction to the reported digits, not to the result. Assert that
no classification moves when you apply it, and if one does, that is a finding rather than a fix.

**The better fix for the denominator, now available and not available before.** D-7 gives you the
full-precision S0 delta, verified to reproduce the stamped 4-dp value. So you do not have to live
with a rounded denominator at all: use the recomputed full-precision S0, with an assertion that it
rounds to the stamped figure. That is the same quantity at more digits, not a different one, and
D-7 is what licenses saying so. It restores a tight `R_TOLERANCE` and removes the cell-dependent
sensitivity your `|5e-5 / delta_full|` formula otherwise introduces — that formula is correct for
the rounded denominator, but it makes A7 roughly 6× weaker on Track B's small-delta cells than on
Track A's, which is honest and also avoidable.

**And the coverage point, which is C-1 one level up.** You wrote that A7 "didn't fire only because
`classify_cell` is called on S2 alone, which is luck rather than design." That is the same defect
C-1 closed: a guard declared over a quantity must run **wherever that quantity is computed**, not
where it happens to be consumed. C-1 fixed branch coverage; this is rung coverage. If `r` is
computed and reported at S0, S1 and S3 — and it is, since you quote the S0 values — then A7 must run
there too. Frozen `halt_conditions` says "any cell," and four rungs × eight cells is the actual
surface. Worth recording as the general form, because it has now recurred once.

One correction of my own to attach: I told you last round that `r > 1.0` is structurally impossible
absent a defect. That was right about the mathematical quantity and wrong about the computed one,
because I did not carry the denominator's rounding through. If you had tightened the tolerance on
the strength of my argument, you would have built the false halt rather than found it. Label it per
A1g alongside the thermal hypothesis.

## 2. The ladder collapsed to two rungs — and that makes the result harder to argue with

`S0 ≡ S1` and `S2 ≡ S3` in outcome means the four-rung ladder has exactly two distinct levels: with
inherited provenance and without. Your verification that the rungs genuinely differ in ranges — 132
of 235 and 184 of 235 — is what makes that a finding rather than a collapsed pipeline, and checking
it rather than assuming it was the right instinct.

Draw out the consequence, because it is the strongest defensive point available to you and it is not
obvious: **the primary scoring turned out to coincide with the hostile floor.** S2 is what carries
the headline, S3 is the most hostile reading anyone could ask for, and they agree on all eight cells.
So the objection "you chose a harsh correction" has no purchase — the harshest available correction
gives the same answer as the primary one. Symmetrically, "you chose a lenient one" fails too, since
S1 ≡ S0.

The whole design question therefore reduces to a single binary: **does inherited provenance count as
a hit?** Every other choice in the ladder turned out to be inert. That is a much cleaner thing to put
in front of a reader than a four-rung table, and it is the sentence I would build B6's methods
section around.

It also sharpens what the paper is missing. The channel that carries the entire measured effect is
the one at `formatted.py:86-94` that the paper never describes. That is not "the effect shrank under
a correction" — it is "the measured benefit on the control was mediated by an indexing behaviour the
paper does not document." Those are different claims and the second is the true one.

## 3. Two things B6 must not do

**Do not let the cell-level detail be phrased as though it modifies the headline.** Two of three
applicable cells are PARTIALLY SEPARATED with corrected deltas above half the original, and one
Track B cell drags the aggregate. That observation is true, it is worth reporting, and it is
*descriptive only* — the least-favourable rule was frozen before any of these numbers existed, and
the entire value of the pre-registration is that this is exactly the moment it binds. Report the
aggregate as governing and the cell detail underneath it, in that order, with no connective that
implies the second qualifies the first. "Family 1 reads NOT SEPARATED. The three applicable cells
were …" and not "… although two of three cells …".

**Do not let the families merge in prose.** The table keeps them apart; readers will merge them
anyway unless the sentences forbid it. Family 1's NOT SEPARATED is a statement about the
size-matched control at 256, and it says nothing about C4. Family 2 says the C4 > C0 claim is **not
established and not refuted** — the correction arm cannot discriminate, which is a weaker and quite
different statement. One explicit sentence saying the two must not be combined is worth more than
any amount of careful table design.

**And the empty family needs its own word.** You report `secondary` as UNDERPOWERED, but its
applicable count is zero — no cell reached applicability at S0 at all, so the correction arm was
never exercised there. Family 2's UNDERPOWERED means an arm ran and could not discriminate. Those
are different states rendering identically, which is the `BLOCKED / ENVIRONMENT` principle exactly:
attempted-and-blocked must never look like never-attempted. Give the empty family a distinct label
— NO APPLICABLE CELLS, or whatever the frozen vocabulary allows — and if the frozen vocabulary does
not allow one, say so in the record rather than reusing a word that means something else.

While you are there: state the **powered** predicate explicitly. I reconstructed it from two data
points (powered = applicable and not UNDERPOWERED) and it holds, but a reader six months out will
have to do the same reconstruction, and it is an §A5 quantity that should be written as one
procedure.

## 5. The silent `str.replace` is the third of its family

`pw1_f1_refers_to` dropped during an A4b fold-in, the NUL bytes from a patch script, now a replace
that matched nothing and printed success. The class is: **a mutation that reports success without
verifying it occurred.** Redoing it with a loudly-failing edit is right; the general rule belongs in
the template beside A1e–A1h, because three instances in a week is a pattern, not a run of bad luck.

The uncomfortable corollary is worth facing while it is cheap. One instance of this class already
reached the stamped freeze undetected. A hash proves the freeze text has not changed since stamping;
it cannot prove the text is complete against what was intended. The one check that would catch a
second silent drop is a **referential-integrity sweep**: extract every identifier, cross-reference
and section pointer in `freeze_text_verbatim`, and assert each one resolves to something that
exists. Cheap, mechanical, and it is the only handle you have on that question. Record the outcome
either way.

## 7. The paper

The two corrections are no longer the whole story. PW1-F1 and PW1-F3 were defects in description;
this is a substantive change to what the evaluation supports. `Semantic_Formatter_WhitePaper_v2.pdf`
is sitting with your approver carrying a headline that the size-matched control no longer separates
from an indexing artifact, and the inheritance channel it doesn't describe is the channel that
carried the effect.

I have said this in four documents now and I will make it short: the note to the approver has gone
from housekeeping to material. An approval obtained this week is an approval of a document you
already know overstates its evidence.

What the paper can still say, accurately, is more than it might feel like right now: the formatter
produces measurable retrieval differences; the practical C4 configuration is not refuted; the
size-matched control's advantage is substantially attributable to inherited provenance rather than
to retrieval; and the evaluation was pre-registered and reported against itself. That last one is
not a consolation prize — a method paper that reports its own control failing is more useful to a
reader than one that doesn't, and materially harder to attack.

**On disclosure**, the calculus has shifted and the decision is still yours. Novelty is unaffected —
a method is novel or not independently of how well it performs. What changed is the value of a
filing versus a defensive publication: defensive publication costs little and requires no efficacy
claim, and it is the route that fits a method whose own control reads NOT SEPARATED. Filing is a
different economics and I am not in a position to price ASML's IP posture. But the decision no
longer needs to wait on PW-1, because PW-1 has concluded.
