# v1.8 G12 — ruling: the custom_id identity is re-encoded over closed vocabularies

**Responds to:** Batch G rejected at create — `custom_id` must match
`^[a-zA-Z0-9_-]{1,64}$`; every id in both tracks illegal; the frozen separator itself
illegal; zero spend; STOPPED correctly under the `29a0fb5` precedent's own terms.
**Date:** 1 August 2026
**Status:** the repair is chosen here, by the ruling side, and recorded as **PF-13** — a
post-freeze amendment made through the STOP-then-rule path the precedent requires when a
fix is not forced. Stopping was right: this fix picks among designs with materially
different properties, and that choice was never the agent's to make.

---

## 1. The ruling: indices into frozen codebooks, so every field is closed and legal

The collision existed because one field — `query_id` — was free text drawn from an alphabet
the acceptor forbids. The repair removes free text from the identity entirely:

> `custom_id = v18-{stage}-{track}-{arm}-q{index}-{metric}-r{rep}`
> e.g. `v18-j-B-u768-q149-ac-r2` (23 chars; worst case well under 64)

- **`{index}`** is the query's zero-padded position in the track's frozen, ordered query
  list. A **codebook** — `query_index.json`, mapping index → full original id — is
  committed with the fix, SHA-256 in the manifest. The codebook is a static, frozen
  artifact: `custom_id` plus codebook jointly constitute the identity, which amends
  PF-12 §2's "sole record" by exactly that much and no more.
- **`{metric}`** comes from a frozen two-letter code table: `cp`, `cr`, `fa`, `ar`, `ac`,
  with a bijection test against the five metric names.
- **`{stage}` ∈ {g, j}, `{track}` ∈ {A, B}, `{arm}` ∈ {u256, u768, f768},
  `{rep}` ∈ {0, 1, 2}** — all closed sets.

Every field now draws from a closed, hyphen-free vocabulary, so the uniform hyphen split
is unambiguous *by construction* — the property the `29a0fb5` fix had to recover by
special-casing is restored structurally, and that fix's both-ends parser is retired along
with the format it patched.

**The acceptance census, mandatory before resubmission:** generate the full cross-product
of custom_ids — every stage, track, arm, query, metric, repeat — and validate every one
against the API's actual pattern and length as a test. Not a sample, not the worst case
found by inspection: the census, against the real acceptor.

## 2. Why not the alternatives

In-place sanitisation fails the length bound (66 > 64 on the worst judge id) and
reinstates parsing ambiguity. Reversible escaping grows length in an alphabet whose only
two punctuation characters both appear in field contents — it deepens the collision it
escapes. A content hash fits and parses but converts the identity into an opaque pointer,
losing the row-level legibility that makes triage and audit possible; the index keeps the
pointer *structured* and the opacity confined to one field with a frozen codebook behind
it. The index is the minimum surrender of PF-12 §2's self-description that the acceptor
permits.

## 3. The orphan-intent defect — fix authorised in this ruling

A synchronous create-rejection leaves an intent that adoption logic conflates with "died
after submitting," so a legitimate retry would STOP. Ruling: intents carry a state —
`submitted` (id pending) or `rejected` (closed) — set from the create call's outcome. Only
`submitted` intents without ids trigger adoption; `rejected` intents permit fresh
submission. Test: a create-rejection marks the intent rejected and a resubmission
proceeds; a genuine post-submit loss still adopts or stops as before. This travels in the
PF-13 commit.

## 4. The class — fourth instance, and the candidate rule sharpens as proposed

E1's provenance model, v1.9's budget, the id-alphabet at `29a0fb5`, and now the acceptor's
pattern: four specifications frozen against imagined domains. The agent's sharpening is
adopted into the queued candidate verbatim in spirit: **exercise every specification
against the real inputs and the real acceptor** — corpus censuses alone would have missed
this one, because the constraint lived in the API, not the data. The self-diagnosis ("I
had the pattern available and validated against half of it") goes with it to the next
pre-registration: a census that stops at the convenient half is the mental-model failure
wearing a census's clothes.
